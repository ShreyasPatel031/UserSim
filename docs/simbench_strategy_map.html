The data

Every row is one population answering one question

SimBench pairs a population description with a multiple-choice question and the real share of that population choosing each option. The model predicts the shares. Two row types matter for strategy: whole-country rows () and subgroup rows () such as one age band inside one country.

Layout is illustrative; the rule is the real one. No answer to the target question may come from any group that could contain the target's respondents. Other countries and disjoint groups in the same country are fine, and so are answers to other questions.

Twenty datasets, two kinds

Five surveys ( questions, of the benchmark) ask many groups in many countries the same questions, so there is a lot of neighboring evidence. The other fifteen datasets are mostly one-off experiments with a single country or no population at all.

What helped, in order

From 41.5 to in fourteen steps

Each bar spans the points one step added to the full-benchmark score. One idea, a two-way decomposition from other groups and countries, accounts for 8.7 of the 21.4 points. Eight tuning rounds on top added 2.9.

Where it stops

The lowest brackets are limited by the data and the rule, not by tuning

Brackets are groups of questions served by one source within one dataset. These are the lowest by score after eight rounds. The oracle numbers use the real answer to choose evidence in hindsight, so they bound what any honest method could reach.
